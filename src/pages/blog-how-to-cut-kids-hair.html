How Do You Cut Kids' Hair and Keep Them Calm in the Chair?

Set up everything before the child sits down, raise them to a comfortable height, and explain each step in simple words. Do the bulk of the cut first while their patience lasts, guide the head gently, and protect the ears and neckline. A calm, predictable barber is what keeps a child still and a family coming back.

Why are kids' haircuts harder than adult cuts?

The fundamentals are the same: clean sectioning, controlled clipper work, sharp lines. What changes is everything around the cut. Attention spans are short, heads move without warning, and the client in the chair isn't the one who booked the appointment.

"With kids, the haircut is the easy part. The calm you bring to the chair is what parents remember."

Before they sit down

How should you set up the chair for a child?

Most of the battle is won before the first pass of the clippers. Have everything ready so you aren't reaching around a wriggling child to find a guard.

Height

Booster seat or board

Raise the child with a booster seat or seat board so you see the head shape clearly and avoid awkward angles.

Comfort

A cape that fits

A kid-sized cape or snug neck strip keeps itchy hair off their skin, which heads off a lot of tears.

Sound

Quiet tools, ready to go

A quieter clipper is less startling near small ears. Lay out guards in order and keep sharp tools out of grabbing range.

Distraction

Something to hold or watch

A toy, a show on a parent's phone, or holding the comb gives restless hands a job. Ask the parent what usually works.

The consultation

What should you ask the parent and tell the child?

A good kids' cut starts with two short conversations, and they're not the same conversation.

With the parent: ask which guard or length they usually go with, whether they want the edges lined up or left soft, and whether anything bothers the child, like clipper noise, the spray bottle or having their ears touched.

With the child: get down to their level, use their name, and explain what's about to happen in simple words. Let them see and hear the clippers first, and narrate as you go: "Now I'm doing the back, now the side." Keep promises small and true; don't promise it won't tickle.

Working the cut

In what order should you cut a child's hair?

Your window of cooperation is limited, so plan the cut so the most important work happens while you still have it.

Priorities

Do the bulk first

Take down length and set the shape early, and save detail work for the end. If patience runs out, you still have a neat, finished-looking cut. Know your guard sequence before you start so you move smoothly.

Breaks

Pause when you need to

If a child is crying hard, stop and let the parent settle them. Sometimes the right call is a simpler cut today and the full style next visit.

Safety around ears and neck

How do you cut safely around a child's ears?

Guide, don't force. A light, steady hand positions the head. Ask the child to look down or tilt instead of pushing, and wait for them to settle before you cut.

Protect the ears. Fold the ear down or cup it with your hand when working around it, move slowly, and keep the clipper blade flat and controlled.

Be careful with open blades. Keep scissor points angled away from the scalp, eyes and ears, and close the shears when you're not cutting. On a wiggly child, clipper-over-comb or a guard is often safer. Many barbers skip the straight razor on young kids and use a trimmer for lineups; follow your shop's policy and the parent's wishes.

"Assume the head will move. Work so that when it does, nothing goes wrong."

A milestone

How do you handle a baby's first haircut?

Let the parent hold the child on their lap if that keeps them calm, with both of you caped. Aim for a tidy shape and clean edges, not a sharp skin fade. Offer to save a small lock of hair as a keepsake and give the parent time for photos. Most of all, keep it relaxed: a first haircut that feels like a fun outing sets the tone for every visit after it. A child who likes their barber at five may still be in your chair at fifteen, and parents often send siblings and friends, the kind of referral-driven book our guide on how much barbers make in New York describes.

What parents ask for

What are the most popular kids' haircuts?

Kids usually want what they see on older siblings, parents and athletes.

Taper and short crop: easy to maintain and grows out well. Low and mid fades: the look older kids see on their dad or big brother, with more coverage than a high skin fade. Caesars and natural shapes: an even Caesar, a shaped-up afro, or a taper that leaves length on top for twists. Scissor trims: for families who keep length and just want a tidy shape and clean neckline.

Simple designs and part lines are popular with older kids too; check with the parent first, since some schools have rules about them. Browse our haircut styles, learn the technique behind cutting a skin fade, or read about training on diverse hair types.

Non-negotiable

Why does sanitation matter even more with kids?

Parents notice a clean station. Disinfected tools and a fresh setup for every client are basic professional standards.

Disinfect clippers, guards, combs and shears between every client and use a fresh neck strip each time. If you notice anything unusual on a child's scalp, don't guess at what it is. Pause, mention it privately and kindly to the parent, and follow your shop's sanitation procedures.

New York requires all license applicants to complete a one-time Transmission of Contagious Diseases course and pass a practical exam, as the NY Department of State barber requirements explain.

Straight answers

Kids' haircuts FAQ

Common questions from barbers and barber students about cutting children's hair.

How do you keep a child still during a haircut?

Set up first so you're not hunting for tools, raise the child on a booster seat, and give them something to hold or watch. Explain each step in simple words, guide the head with a light hand, and do the most important work early while they're still patient.

What clippers are best for cutting kids' hair?

Many barbers prefer a quieter clipper or trimmer for young children, since loud buzzing near the ears can be startling. The best tool is one you can control smoothly and confidently.

What should a barber do if a child gets upset mid-cut?

Stop and step back. Let the parent comfort the child, take a short break, and then decide together whether to finish or keep it simple. A clean, basic cut today beats pushing through with a moving head.

Do barber students learn to cut kids' hair?

The skills behind a great kids' cut, like fades, tapers, scissor work, safe implement handling and sanitation, are core parts of barber training. ABI's 500-hour Master Barber program builds them on mannequins first, then on real clients on a supervised clinic floor.
