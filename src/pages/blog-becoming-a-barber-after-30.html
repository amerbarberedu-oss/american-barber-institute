Is It Too Late to Become a Barber After 30?

No. New York sets a minimum age of 17 for a barber license and no maximum, so the path is the same at 30, 40 or 50. At ABI you complete a 500-hour program in about 4 months full-time or 6–7 months on weekends, then pass the state practical exam. You need no degree or experience, just a plan for money, time and family while you train.

Why people switch

Why do people switch to barbering in their 30s and 40s?

Usually they have spent years in a job that pays the bills but doesn't feel like theirs, and they want work that is skilled, social and in their own hands.

Some have hit a ceiling and want income that grows with skill instead of a manager's approval. Many have cut friends' hair for years and want to do it properly, with a license. Barbering fits because the entry point is short and defined, the training is hands-on, and the career has a clear ladder: commission, often booth rent, and for some a shop of their own.

"You're not starting over. You're starting a new trade with everything you already know about people, money and showing up."

Paying the bills

How do you pay the bills while you're in barber school?

You don't have to stop earning or pay for school in one lump sum.

Keep your paycheck on the weekend track. Plan C (Saturday and Sunday) covers the same 500 hours in about 6–7 months while you keep a weekday job. The full-time tracks, morning (Plan A, Monday to Friday, 8 AM–2 PM) and afternoon (Plan B, 2 PM–8 PM), finish in about 4 months and leave half the day free for part-time work or family.

Pay as you go. Plan B is $3,600, Plan C is $4,600, and Plan A is $5,600 with a professional tool kit included. Each starts with a $200–$500 down payment, including a $100 non-refundable registration fee, followed by weekly payments while you train.

Check your funding. Veterans may be able to use GI Bill® benefits; see our GI Bill guide. New Yorkers whose disability affects their ability to work may qualify for ACCES-VR; see the ACCES-VR guide. The full breakdown is on tuition & funding.

After the license

What does your first year as a barber look like?

Most new barbers start on commission, splitting each ticket with the shop in exchange for walk-ins, products and a place to learn. New York graduates building a book typically earn in the $30K–$45K range, according to our barber income guide, and that rises as you get faster and fill your schedule with regulars.

Use slow days to post your work and ask happy clients for referrals. Career-changers often have an edge: a network of friends and former coworkers who need haircuts. Once your book is full, our guide to booth rent vs. commission explains when a switch makes sense.

Straight talk

What are the hardest parts of switching careers?

Income dip

You may earn less at first

There is no guaranteed wage in this trade. A savings buffer and a commission chair make the early months easier.

Physical work

You're on your feet all day

Good shoes, a properly adjusted chair, stretching between clients and good posture matter more every year you cut.

Being the beginner

Learning next to younger classmates

Your maturity is an asset with clients, and the craft rewards practice, not age.

"Clients care about the cut in the mirror. Nobody in the chair asks how old you were when you started."

Straight answers

Career-changer FAQ

Am I too old to become a barber at 40 or 50?

No. New York requires applicants to be at least 17 but sets no maximum age. The requirements are the same at any age: complete training at an approved school, file the application with a health certification and the one-time contagious diseases course, and pass the NYS practical exam.

Can I keep my job while going to barber school?

Yes. ABI's weekend track (Plan C) covers the same 500 hours in about 6–7 months while you keep a weekday job. The weekday tracks take about 4 months.

Is there a written exam for the New York barber license?

The state requires passing the NYS practical examination, which must be passed within two years. Barbers licensed in Maine, New Mexico or Pennsylvania may qualify for reciprocity, which can waive the practical.

How can I afford barber school as an adult with bills?

ABI's plans run from $3,600 to $5,600 and start with a $200–$500 down payment, then weekly payments while you train. GI Bill® and ACCES-VR funding are accepted.

What will I earn in my first year as a barber?

It depends on speed, pricing, location and how quickly you build regulars. New graduates building a book in New York typically earn in the $30K–$45K range, rising as the book fills.
