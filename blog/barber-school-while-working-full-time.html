Can You Go to Barber School While Working Full-Time?

By Truth "The Barber Artist" Quinones · Founding Director, ABI Bronx · November 2, 2026

Yes. At American Barber Institute you can complete the 500-hour Master Barber program on weekends (about 6–7 months) or on a morning or afternoon weekday track (about 4 months) while you keep your job. Tuition is paid with a small down payment and weekly payments, so it can come out of the paycheck you're already earning.

Read this first

Does training part-time change your license?

No. Every ABI schedule covers the same 500 hours of theory, mannequin work and supervised clinic-floor time with real clients, and every one prepares you for the same New York State barber license.

What changes is the pace. A weekday track finishes faster; the weekend track takes longer but leaves your weekdays free for work. The right schedule is the one you can actually show up for, week after week, until your hours are done.

"Employers care that you're licensed and skilled, not which schedule you used to get there."

Step 1

Which barber school schedule fits a full-time job?

ABI runs three tracks for the same 500-hour program. Here's how each one lines up against a typical work week.

Track Class hours Time to finish Works best if you…
Morning (Plan A)Mon–Fri, 8 AM–2 PM~4 monthsWork afternoons, evenings or nights
Afternoon (Plan B)Mon–Fri, 2 PM–8 PM~4 monthsWork early mornings or overnight shifts
Weekend (Plan C)Sat & Sun~6–7 monthsHold a standard Monday–Friday day job

If you work a classic 9-to-5, the weekend track is usually the cleanest fit: your weekday job stays untouched. If you work nights, early-morning delivery, or restaurant and retail shifts, one half of the weekday may be genuinely free, and a weekday track can get you finished in about four months.

New classes start the first Monday of every month (the Tuesday after, when that Monday is a federal holiday), so you're rarely more than a few weeks from a start date. The schedules page lists every start date and holiday closure.

Step 2

How do you pay for barber school while working?

Keeping your job means tuition can come out of income you already have. At ABI, nobody pays the full cost before day one.

How it works

Down payment, then weekly

Every plan starts with a $200–$500 down payment at registration (including a $100 non-refundable registration fee), then weekly payments while you attend.

The plans

Three prices, one program

Plan B (afternoon) is $3,600, Plan C (weekend) is $4,600, and Plan A (morning) is $5,600 with a professional tool kit included. Confirm your exact weekly amount with admissions.

Lower the cost

Funding you may qualify for

Veterans may be able to use GI Bill® benefits, and eligible New Yorkers may qualify for ACCES-VR vocational funding.

Treat your weekly payment like a fixed bill and set it aside the day you get paid. Leave room near the end for the state's fees, which are paid to New York, not the school: $40 for the initial license application and $15 for the practical exam. Our full cost guide and the tuition & funding page lay out every line.

Step 3

Should you tell your employer?

You don't owe anyone an explanation, but in most jobs a short, honest conversation makes the next few months smoother. Present it as a schedule you'll protect, not a problem they have to solve.

Go in knowing exactly which hours you need free. On the weekend track, you may only need to ask not to be scheduled on Saturdays and Sundays. On a weekday track, ask for a fixed shift on the other side of class time instead of a rotating one. If your schedule can't bend, that's useful information too: the weekend track may be the better choice for now, or you can start a month later once a different shift is lined up.

"Pick the schedule you can keep. Consistency over the full 500 hours matters more than how fast you finish."

Step 4

How do you practice between classes?

Weekend and part-day students have more days between sessions, and more time for skills to fade. A little practice in between keeps your hands from starting over.

Work on a mannequin head at home. Twenty or thirty minutes on a weeknight is enough to rehearse sectioning, guard changes and clipper angles. Focus on one thing per session, like blending a single guard line cleanly.

Drill your sanitation routine. Setting up your station and disinfecting implements in the right order is a habit you can practice anywhere, and it matters on the practical exam.

Protect your sleep. Tired hands make sloppy lines. Pack your kit the night before, use the train ride to review theory, and set a bedtime around class that you keep.

Keep notes after every class. Write down what your instructor corrected and what to try next time, so you pick up exactly where you left off. Still building your kit? Our beginner barber tool kit guide covers what you'll actually use.

Step 5

What do you need for the New York barber license?

Finishing your hours is only part of the goal. To get the state barber license, you must pass the NYS practical examination, administered by the New York Department of State, within two years.

Applicants also need a health certification from a physician, physician's assistant or nurse practitioner, and must complete a one-time Transmission of Contagious Diseases course, which ABI offers as a home-study course by mail. File your paperwork as soon as you're eligible. The official NY Department of State barber requirements list every step, and our guide to passing the New York barber state board exam and the NY licensing checklist walk through it in detail.

Straight answers

Working while in barber school: FAQ

The questions working adults ask most before they enroll.

Can I go to barber school while working a full-time job?

Yes. ABI offers morning (Mon–Fri, 8 AM–2 PM), afternoon (Mon–Fri, 2 PM–8 PM) and weekend tracks. If you work a standard weekday job, the weekend track (Plan C) lets you keep it while you complete the same 500-hour program.

How long does barber school take on a weekend schedule?

ABI's weekend track takes about 6–7 months to complete the 500-hour program. The morning and afternoon tracks take about 4 months. Holiday closures can add a little time, so build in a small buffer.

Do I have to pay tuition all at once?

No. Every plan starts with a $200–$500 down payment at registration, then weekly payments while you train. Plan B (afternoon) is $3,600, Plan C (weekend) is $4,600 and Plan A (morning) is $5,600 with a tool kit included.

What exam do I need to pass to get a barber license in New York?

New York requires you to pass the NYS practical examination within two years. You also need to be 17 or older, submit a health certification, and complete a one-time Transmission of Contagious Diseases course.

When can I start?

New classes begin the first Monday of every month, or the Tuesday after when that Monday is a federal holiday. The schedules page lists exact start dates so you can line up your work schedule ahead of time.
